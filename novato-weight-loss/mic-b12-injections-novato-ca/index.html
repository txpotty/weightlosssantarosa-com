    How MIC B12 Injections Work for Patients in Novato, CA

    What Is in an MIC B12 Injection in Novato

    MIC is an acronym. The M is methionine, the I is inositol and the C is choline, nutrients involved in how the liver packages and exports fat, and the mix is combined with vitamin B12 as cyanocobalamin or methylcobalamin. Because a compounding pharmacy mixes it, the combination has no FDA approval. Think of it as nutritional support rather than a weight loss drug.

    Who MIC B12 Is For

    Patients on GLP-1s eat less. Plenty of people asking about MIC B12 are on semaglutide or tirzepatide and eating far smaller meals, and some also take metformin, which can lower B12 levels with long-term use. Vegans and adults over 50 run low more often too. It does not replace a GLP-1.

    Dosing and Injection Schedule in Novato

    Most plans use one weekly shot. You inject into the outer thigh or upper arm muscle with a small needle, rotating sides each week so one spot does not get sore, and some formulas are labeled for subcutaneous use in the belly instead. Some providers start with two shots a week for the first month. Check the label for storage, since many MIC B12 vials need refrigeration.

    What to Expect Week by Week

    Week one is mostly about technique. Patients who were low on B12 sometimes notice more energy within two to four weeks, while people with normal levels often feel no difference at all, which is a useful answer in itself. The scale moves because of diet and any GLP-1, not because of the shot. Your provider checks in around week four to ask whether it is worth continuing.

    Side Effects and Safety in Novato

    Sore arms are the usual complaint. Mild redness at the injection site or a short bout of loose stools can follow a shot, and both usually fade within a day or so of the injection. Serious allergic reactions are rare, though they do happen. Swelling of the face or throat after a shot needs emergency care, not a message to your provider.

    Eligibility and Screening

    Allergy questions come first. People with a cobalt or cobalamin allergy should not use B12 injections, and cyanocobalamin is avoided in Leber hereditary optic neuropathy because it can worsen optic nerve damage. Kidney disease and polycythemia get a closer review. During pregnancy or breastfeeding, your provider weighs it case by case. Suspected deficiency may mean a B12 level before anything else.

    How MIC B12 Compares to GLP-1 Treatment in Novato

    The evidence gap is large. Semaglutide produced about 15 percent average weight loss over 68 weeks in the STEP 1 trial, and tirzepatide reached roughly 20 percent at the top dose in SURMOUNT-1, while MIC B12 has no trial showing meaningful weight loss. So it is usually an add-on, not a standalone plan. Patients who want weight loss medication are steered toward a GLP-1 review instead.
