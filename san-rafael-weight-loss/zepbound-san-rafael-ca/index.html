    How Zepbound Treatment Works in San Rafael, CA

    How Zepbound Works in San Rafael

    Tirzepatide acts on two gut hormone receptors. By activating both the GIP and GLP-1 receptors, it slows stomach emptying, signals fullness to the brain and helps the body manage blood sugar after eating. Most people feel full on smaller portions. It is injected under the skin of the stomach, thigh or upper arm once a week.

    Who Zepbound Is For

    Zepbound is approved for adults with a BMI of 30 or higher. It is also approved for adults with a BMI of 27 or higher who have a weight-related condition such as high blood pressure, high cholesterol, type 2 diabetes or sleep apnea. A separate approval covers moderate to severe obstructive sleep apnea in adults with obesity. It is not approved for children.

    Zepbound Dosing Schedule in San Rafael

    Everyone starts at 2.5 mg. That first month is about letting your stomach adjust, not about weight loss, so modest early changes are normal. From there, doses climb in 2.5 mg steps every four weeks or more, through 5, 7.5, 10 and 12.5 mg to a top of 15 mg. Most people settle at 5 mg, 10 mg or 15 mg.

    What to Expect Week by Week in San Rafael

    The first month is mostly quiet. You may notice a smaller appetite within the first week, and stomach upset tends to cluster in the day or two after a shot or a dose increase. In the SURMOUNT-1 trial, adults on the highest dose lost about 20 percent of body weight on average over 72 weeks. Individual results vary.

    Zepbound Side Effects

    Nausea and diarrhea come up most. Vomiting, constipation, indigestion, stomach pain and redness at the injection site are also common, especially during the first days after each dose increase. Serious but less common risks include pancreatitis, gallbladder problems, kidney injury from dehydration and severe allergic reactions. Zepbound carries a boxed warning about thyroid C-cell tumors seen in rodents.

    Eligibility and Screening in San Rafael

    Your provider starts with height, weight and BMI. They then go through your history and medications, looking hardest at insulin, glipizide-type drugs and oral contraceptives. People with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN 2 cannot take Zepbound, and it is not used during pregnancy. Labs may be requested.

    Zepbound Compared With Related Options in San Rafael

    Zepbound and Mounjaro are the same medicine. The only real difference is the approved use, weight for Zepbound and type 2 diabetes for Mounjaro, which affects coverage and paperwork. In the SURMOUNT-5 head-to-head trial, tirzepatide produced more average weight loss than Wegovy. Compounded tirzepatide is not FDA-approved and is a different product from Zepbound.
