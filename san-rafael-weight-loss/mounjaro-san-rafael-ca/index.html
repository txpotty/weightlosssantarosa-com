    How Mounjaro Treatment Works in San Rafael, CA

    How Mounjaro Works in San Rafael

    Tirzepatide activates two receptors at once. Through the GIP and GLP-1 receptors, it prompts insulin release when glucose is high, lowers glucagon and keeps food in the stomach longer. Appetite often drops as a result. The same molecule sold as Zepbound carries the weight management label.

    Who Mounjaro Is For

    Mounjaro is approved for adults with type 2 diabetes. It is used along with diet and exercise to improve blood sugar control, and it is not approved for type 1 diabetes or for weight loss in people without diabetes. If you do not have diabetes, your provider will likely suggest Zepbound instead. Off-label use is disclosed when it happens.

    Mounjaro Dosing Schedule in San Rafael

    The first four weeks are at 2.5 mg. That dose is a starter meant to help your body adjust, and it is not expected to control blood sugar on its own. After that it moves to 5 mg, then up in 2.5 mg steps no faster than every four weeks, as high as 15 mg. Your glucose response decides each step.

    What to Expect Week by Week

    Early weeks bring smaller portions and fewer snacks. Nausea or loose stools are most likely in the day or two after starting and after each increase, and they usually settle within a week as your body adjusts. Blood sugar readings often improve within the first month or two. The A1C drawn around month three shows the real trend, and the SURPASS clinical trials reported average reductions near 2 points at higher doses.

    Mounjaro Side Effects in San Rafael

    Nausea is the most common complaint. Diarrhea, decreased appetite, vomiting, constipation, indigestion and stomach pain also show up, mostly in the early weeks and after each dose increase. Less common serious risks include pancreatitis, gallbladder problems, kidney injury from dehydration and worsening diabetic retinopathy. Mounjaro has a boxed warning for thyroid C-cell tumors seen in rodents.

    Eligibility and Screening

    Your provider looks first at your type 2 diabetes diagnosis and a recent A1C. After that come your current medications, weight, kidney history and any family history of thyroid cancer, since each one changes the answer. People with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN 2 should not use Mounjaro. Pregnancy does too.

    Mounjaro Compared With Related Options in San Rafael

    Same medicine, two labels. Mounjaro is approved for type 2 diabetes and Zepbound for weight management, and that difference affects insurance coverage and how your provider writes the prescription. Ozempic, by contrast, is semaglutide and works through the GLP-1 receptor only. Compounded tirzepatide is not FDA-approved and is a different product from Mounjaro.
